## Feasibility and cost gate

The 2026-10-13 paired capture returned 20,175 catalog SKUs and 935 shopper SKUs; all shopper IDs appeared in the catalog with matching displayed prices and packs. The catalog crawl took about 25.5 minutes at a minimum 1.25-second request interval and occupied ~17 MB of local disk; the shopper capture occupied ~812 KB. Structured HTML needs no paid vision model. This makes a read-only, weekly Pueblo study relatively low-cost, but the audit found other promotions outside the shopper, item-specific validity, unavailable items, and ambiguous name/pack groups. The 19,240 catalog-only SKUs are an *upper bound on additional identifiers*, not 19,240 verified regular-priced, in-stock, usable Mi Lista products. No production migration is justified by this capture alone.

## Collection and provenance

- `spikes/001-pueblo-catalog/research.py` checks the *printed shopper edition* and captures complete `type=shopper` and `type=all` feeds into separate, resumable, checksummed, gitignored folders only when it changes. It compares the new pair with the previous fully compared edition; a shared lock prevents a manual run from colliding with the initial one-off timer. A partial crawl, future-dated edition, or edition change mid-crawl is refused. Week comparison JSON does **not** establish a regular price.
- The existing one-off follow-up is scheduled for October 15 with October 16 fallback. The recurring study timer checks Thu/Fri/Sun at 11:00 AST to continue the same read-only capture thereafter; an unchanged edition costs only the shopper-page check. On every scheduled run the offline evaluator writes `data/catalog-pilots/study_evaluation.json`; neither job writes to `offers`, the API, user lists, Supabase, or Netlify. `Persistent=true` and user linger catch boots/logins, but a workstation powered down or booted into Windows across an entire shopper edition cannot recover that missing full catalog retrospectively.
- A missing week is a gap, not silently interpolated history. Keep the raw HTML/page hashes and edition manifests; spot-check a stratified detail sample each new edition for availability, promotion status, pack and price. Shopper absence does not prove non-promo pricing. Store/channel and pickup location remain unverified for physical locations.

## Evaluation contract

1. Freeze source, store/channel, item SKU, pack and quantities *before* a forecast cutoff. The first paired edition is a starting observation, not a held-out result. Do not use generated investor data, archived OCR-only flyer rows, a random row split, or future prices at training time. Use only consecutive, seven-day-separated complete pairs; abstain when a pack changes, SKU vanishes, source freshness fails, or next price is unavailable.
2. `evaluate.py` currently scores two transparent **item-level research baselines** against the following edition's displayed online price: last observed price and median of prices seen strictly before that target. It begins reporting cutoffs only after four prior consecutive editions, resetting on a missing-week gap. Per-cutoff item MAE, comparable-item count and advertised-target count are diagnostic, **not** shopping-timing success rates. Even with 8 editions and 4 consecutive evaluable cutoffs, the report only says `baseline_review_possible` and explicitly keeps `prediction_release_approved=false`.
3. Before testing a personalized recommendation, obtain a small set of real, consented or explicitly volunteered frozen baskets with SKU, pack, quantity, preferred stores and cadence. Do not collect account behavioral events for this without the consent/policy work in Sprint 4. Compare against buy-now at the best *currently verified* deal, last-observed-price and median-price baselines, and a candidate fitted only to earlier editions. Evaluate coverage, item/basket absolute price error, correct buy/wait decisions, abstention, and realized regret **only when both options were independently observable for the same basket/channel**. Do not call an unknowable counterfactual a realized saving.
4. Proposed review entry: about 8–12 consecutive paired editions and at least four untouched, chronological evaluation cutoffs, plus auditable basket coverage and a predeclared, owner-approved material improvement threshold after extraction/QA costs. This is a study design, not a claim of accuracy. If the candidate does not beat simple baselines, return no-go/insufficient history. A weekly source cannot validate the best *day*; a season of weekly data cannot establish the best *month*.
5. Only after a defensible result and completed-profile eligibility enforced **server-side** should a real estimate be considered next to observed history, with a range, horizon, source freshness and «Estimación basada en precios observados, no una garantía ni una mirada al futuro». Synthetic prototype approval is a separate presentation decision, not scientific validation.
